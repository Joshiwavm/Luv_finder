Mosaics and the primary beam¶

Data layout¶

DataHandler holds one Chunk per (field, spectral window): the weighted visibilities X = w * V and the weight of every visibility w (0 where flagged) as (n_chan, n_row) arrays, u, v, time, baseline and scan per row and frequency per channel. The weights are Stokes I of WEIGHT_SPECTRUM (or WEIGHT), corrected on reading per scan and channel by the noise measured from pairs of each baseline’s integrations. Autocorrelations and flagged rows are dropped.

Positions (dra, ddec) are arcsec east and north of one reference direction per dataset, the phase centre of its first target field. Every chunk knows its own field’s phase centre in that frame, so a grid point is the same sky position in every pointing, and the default position grid is a lattice anchored at the reference.

Searching a mosaic¶

A mosaic is searched one pointing at a time, on one sky grid. search_pointings() loads each field of an NPZ or measurement set in turn, searches it, corrects it for its primary beam and combines the pointings:

from luv_finder.matchedfilter import search_pointings

result = search_pointings("mosaic.npz", {"width": [200.0, 300.0]}, jackknife=True)

Each pointing covers the region where its primary beam is at least pb_limit (utils.PB_LIMIT, 0.2). A dataset with a single field is primary-beam corrected too, so everything downstream sees PB-corrected results. The steps are available separately as build_grid(), MatchedFilter.run, pb_corrected() and combine_pointings().

From the command line:

luv-find --ms mosaic.ms --grid grid.yaml --jackknife --out mosaic_response.npz
luv-find --ms mosaic.ms --field 1 --grid grid.yaml --out field1.npz

luv-find goes through search_pointings: every field, or the one --field names, PB-corrected and combined, and reports the best grid point. A run does the same for its catalogue.

The primary beam¶

luv_finder.utils.primary_beam() is an Airy pattern scaled to the FWHM the ALMA Technical Handbook gives for the real antennas, 1.13 lambda/D, its series near the pointing centre so the fit’s derivatives hold there. Dividing by it leaves a pointing’s S/N unchanged; the combination weights every pointing by PB^2/sigma^2, so overlaps gain S/N, and a pointing contributes nothing where its PB is below pb_limit.

Dirty cubes and maps¶

For inspection, luv_finder.imaging makes natural-weighted dirty cubes on the search grid by type-1 NUFFT (dirty_cube()), moment-8 and continuum maps (dirty_maps()) and their linear mosaic (mosaic_dirty_maps()). The search does not use them.
